EEG Microstate Connectivity Pipeline

Private bachelor-thesis portfolio project on resting-state EEG microstates, functional connectivity, and graph-theoretical analysis in healthy controls and people with multiple sclerosis.

Aim

Starting from private high-density resting-state EEG recordings, the underlying MATLAB-oriented pipeline extracts and backfits microstates, estimates microstate-specific functional connectivity, and derives graph-theoretical measures.

The project aims to investigate exploratory group differences across combinations of frequency band, microstate class, and graph-theoretical feature. It is not intended to validate a clinical biomarker; its primary contribution is an integrated, technically transparent analysis workflow.

Project Overview

The pipeline combines microstate extraction and backfitting, orthogonalized amplitude-envelope correlation, edge-wise rank-sum comparisons, graph-based summaries, and exploratory group-level statistics.

1. Microstate extraction and backfitting

Resting-state EEG is processed with EEGLAB and MICROSTATELAB. Individual microstate maps are extracted at Global Field Power peaks with k-means clustering. Group and grand-level templates are constructed, then sorted against the Koenig 2002 template to assign comparable microstate labels across subjects and groups.

The shared template is backfit to subject-level EEG data, assigning a microstate label to each relevant time sample. Temporal parameters—including individual explained variance, mean duration, mean occurrence, and coverage—are subsequently compared between groups.

2. Microstate-specific connectivity

The microstate-labelled data are converted to FieldTrip format. For each subject, frequency band, and microstate, the pipeline selects the relevant samples and estimates functional connectivity with orthogonalized amplitude-envelope correlation (AEC).

The method uses Hilbert-derived amplitude envelopes and directional orthogonalization for each channel pair to reduce the influence of zero-lag correlations related to volume conduction. The two directional correlations are averaged to produce a symmetric channel-by-channel connectivity matrix.

3. Edge-wise and graph-theoretical analysis

Each connectivity matrix is vectorized over its upper triangle for edge-wise group comparison using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Subject-level weighted connectivity matrices are also used to calculate nodal and global graph measures, including centrality, clustering coefficient, global efficiency, characteristic path length, modularity, and assortativity.

Connectivity matrices are organized by anatomical channel region for interpretation. Nodal measures are summarized across channels, then compared between groups with Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and exploratory ROC/AUC analysis.

Requirements

The underlying analysis pipeline requires:

  • MATLAB;
  • EEGLAB and MICROSTATELAB;
  • FieldTrip;
  • Brain Connectivity Toolbox functions;
  • the original private EEG data, channel mask, coordinate file, and compatible MATLAB toolbox environment.

Implementation

The underlying implementation is organized around an upstream microstate-processing workflow and a downstream ordered analysis workflow. The upstream stage loads subject-level EEG data, applies channel selection, performs microstate clustering and backfitting, converts results to FieldTrip structures, and checkpoint-saves orthogonalized AEC connectivity results.

The downstream stage analyzes temporal parameters, performs edge-wise connectivity statistics, computes graph metrics, ranks graph-derived features, and generates visualizations. Supporting routines handle subject-level AEC computation, anatomical channel ordering, connectivity normalization, and BrainNet-compatible visualization outputs.

Results

The associated report describes exploratory differences that were mainly related to betweenness centrality. However, the principal exploratory findings did not survive FDR correction and must be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

The portfolio intentionally presents only synthetic values and illustrative figures. No raw data, participant-level results, connectivity matrices, or experimental numerical outputs are included.
